# AI-Augmented Second Brains / PKM: State of the Field (2023–2026)

Research report for building an AI-augmented read-later app as the capture layer of a BASB-style second brain. Sources are web research current to July 2026.

---

## 1. The market moment for read-later apps

The capture-tool landscape was violently reshaped in 2024–2025: **Omnivore** (the beloved open-source read-later app) shut its cloud service in Nov 2024 after being acqui-hired by ElevenLabs, and **Pocket** was killed by Mozilla in 2025 ([readless.app/blog/omnivore-alternatives-2026](https://www.readless.app/blog/omnivore-alternatives-2026), [readless.app/blog/best-read-later-apps-comparison](https://www.readless.app/blog/best-read-later-apps-comparison)). Survivors/new entrants: **Readwise Reader** (~$9.99/mo, the feature-for-feature leader), **Instapaper**, **Matter** (strong free tier, newsletters/RSS), **Wallabag** (self-hosted, no AI), plus a wave of AI-first read-later apps: **Recall** (recall.it / getrecall.ai), **Readless** (AI newsletter digests), **Screvi**, **Glasp**, **Otio** (AI research workspace), **Burn 451** (AI-first with a 24-hour expiry forcing function and MCP access to your reading history). This is a real gap: there is currently no dominant open, hackable, AI-native read-later app — that's the hole Omnivore left.

**Readwise Reader** is the reference design. Its AI layer, **Ghostreader**, embeds GPT-based prompts directly in the reading flow: summarize document, define term-in-context, explain concept, translate, Q&A against the doc/PDF, and fully user-customizable prompt templates; it recently moved from a prompt-button model to a unified document-aware chat ([docs.readwise.io/reader/guides/ghostreader/overview](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/guides/ghostreader/overview)). Reader's non-AI core is equally instructive: triage states (inbox / later / archive / shortlist), every content type in one queue (articles, PDFs, EPUBs, newsletters via a dedicated email address, RSS, YouTube with transcript, Twitter threads), highlights that flow into a daily spaced-repetition review, and export integrations to Obsidian/Notion. In 2025 Readwise shipped an official **MCP server** (`https://mcp2.readwise.io/mcp`) exposing full-text + semantic search over highlights and Reader docs to Claude/ChatGPT/Cursor, with actions (archive, tag, create highlight) — canonical example of "reading history as agent-accessible context" ([readwise.io/mcp](https://readwise.io/mcp)).

**Recall** ([recall.it](https://www.recall.it/)) is the closest existing product to "AI-augmented read-later as second brain": save article/YouTube/podcast/PDF → AI summary on capture → auto-categorization into a self-organizing knowledge graph (entities auto-linked) → chat over your saved corpus (your knowledge, the web, or both) → **spaced-repetition quizzes auto-generated from saves**. 500k+ users. Its pitch line — "AI gave everyone the same brain, Recall gives AI yours" — captures the emerging positioning.

**Burn 451**'s practitioner-oriented analysis of which read-later AI features matter is worth internalizing ([burn451.cloud/blog/ai-read-later](https://www.burn451.cloud/blog/ai-read-later)): read-later apps fail because they optimize *saving*, not *reading* (one cited user: 1,200 Pocket saves, 180 read). Features with real impact: **AI summaries for triage** (decide in 30s whether an article deserves your time), **semantic search by concept**, and **MCP/API access to reading history from your AI tools**. Gimmicks: keyword search rebranded as AI, tag suggestions without behavioral change, highlight systems without a retrieval/resurfacing mechanism. Their architecture note: separate the saving pipeline from the reading workflow; "summaries + forced decisions outperform frictionless saving."

## 2. AI-native note tools — what each bet on

- **Mem / Mem 2.0** ([get.mem.ai/blog/introducing-mem-2-0](https://get.mem.ai/blog/introducing-mem-2-0)) — the purest "AI-first, zero-organization" bet: no folders/tags; AI auto-connects related notes; **Agentic Chat** that doesn't just answer but creates/edits/organizes notes ("tell Mem something new and it updates or creates a note"); Deep Search (semantic); "Heads Up" proactive resurfacing of relevant notes (e.g., during meetings); voice-to-structured-note. Relaunched Oct 2025 with offline support. Historically criticized for retrieval quality when the corpus is messy — the "self-organizing" promise repeatedly under-delivered in v1.
- **Tana** ([tana.inc/docs/tana-ai](https://tana.inc/docs/tana-ai)) — "an outliner that took AI seriously from day one." **Supertags** attach typed schema (fields, dates, references) to any node, and AI commands hang off supertags — so AI behavior is driven by data structure, not free text. 2025 additions: Meeting Agent (live transcription in 61 languages, extracts action items and routes them to the right context), mobile AI voice chat that can search your workspace and the web mid-conversation, and workspaces "open to both people and AI agents to query" ([outliner.tana.inc — What's new in Tana 2025](https://outliner.tana.inc/articles/whats-new-in-tana-2025-product-updates)). Key lesson: **structure (typed tags) makes AI reliable; AI over unstructured soup is flaky.**
- **Notion AI Q&A** — enterprise-scale proof of RAG-over-workspace. Architecture: per-page/block embeddings via OpenAI zero-retention embedding API → stored in **Turbopuffer** (migrated their multi-billion-vector workload there in late 2024, 10x scale at 1/10th cost vs. their prior pod architecture) → retrieval + LLM answer with source links; also federates connectors (Slack, Google Drive) ([notion.com/blog/two-years-of-vector-search-at-notion](https://www.notion.com/blog/two-years-of-vector-search-at-notion)).
- **Reflect** — daily-notes graph journaling with a deliberately thin AI layer (GPT-based: transcribe voice notes, summarize, chat with notes); pitched at people who want AI "added on top, not bolted on."
- **Capacities** — object/type-based PKM (notes are typed objects, not files in folders); AI assistant works within that object model. Same lesson as Tana: types give AI handles.
- **RemNote** — notes + built-in spaced repetition; AI generates flashcards from notes/PDFs. The main learning-focused PKM.
- **mymind** ([mymind.com](https://mymind.com/)) — the "save it and forget it" capture archetype: save anything (article, image, tweet, PDF), AI reads it, understands the content type, auto-tags it; **no folders, no manual tags at all**; retrieval is 100% search ("type what you remember — keywords, colors, brands"). Deliberately private/anti-social. Proof that AI auto-tagging + good search can fully replace filing for a capture tool.
- **Otio** ([otio.ai](https://otio.ai/)) — AI-native research workspace: collect PDFs/links/notes/audio/video into Spaces → AI-generated notes + source-grounded Q&A with tappable citations → draft outputs from your sources. Multi-model (GPT/Claude/Gemini/DeepSeek). Effectively "read-later for researchers."

Market structure per 2026 roundups ([tana.inc/blog/best-second-brain-apps-2026](https://tana.inc/blog/best-second-brain-apps-2026), [storyflow.so/blog/best-ai-second-brain-apps-2026](https://storyflow.so/blog/best-ai-second-brain-apps-2026)): the category split into lanes — text-stream capture-heavy (Mem), structured power-PKM (Tana, Capacities), daily-notes (Reflect), database (Notion), canvas (Heptabase), local-first (Obsidian).

## 3. Obsidian AI ecosystem

- **Smart Connections** (brianpetro) — the most-installed Obsidian AI plugin. Ships a **local embedding model (transformers.js-based), zero config, no API key**; notes are chunked into `smart_sources`/`smart_blocks`, embedded, and the plugin surfaces "related notes" in a live pane while you write + semantic search + Smart Chat. Built as a monorepo of 15+ packages around a shared "Smart Environment" that keeps embeddings in sync via Obsidian file events ([github.com/brianpetro/obsidian-smart-connections](https://github.com/brianpetro/obsidian-smart-connections), [deepwiki.com/brianpetro/obsidian-smart-connections](https://deepwiki.com/brianpetro/obsidian-smart-connections)). Its killer feature is *ambient, passive* connection-surfacing — no query needed.
- **Copilot for Obsidian** (logancyang) — the broadest: chat panel with vault-wide RAG ("Vault QA"), inline generation/rewrite, agentic tool-calling loops that can edit files. Architecture (from [deepwiki.com/logancyang/obsidian-copilot](https://deepwiki.com/logancyang/obsidian-copilot)): pluggable index backends (**Orama** embedded JS vector/hybrid store, JSONL persistence), retriever selection between hybrid vector, BM25+ lexical (`TieredLexicalRetriever`), and a layered L1–L5 "prompt envelope" context assembler pulling notes, PDFs, web tabs, YouTube transcripts, Dataview queries. Supports cloud + local (Ollama/LM Studio) models. GitHub issues show the recurring pain point: **embedded JS vector stores breaking on large vaults** (Orama string-size limits, index corruption) — a warning if you build in-process vector search in Node.
- **Text Generator** — template-driven prompt/completion inside notes; the "AI as typing accelerator" archetype.
- Recommended community setup 2026: Smart Connections + Copilot on a local Ollama backend covers ~80% of second-brain AI use (semantic search + chat) fully offline; **nomic-embed-text (768d)** is the consensus local embedding model for balance of quality/speed, mxbai-embed-large (1024d) slightly better but 3x slower ([promptquorum.com](https://www.promptquorum.com/power-local-llm/local-llm-with-obsidian-2026)).
- **The 2025–26 shift: from plugins to agents-over-files.** Multiple Obsidian **MCP servers** (MCPVault with 14 methods incl. frontmatter-safe patches and BM25 search; obsidian-ai-mcp which reads a `vault_context.md` at startup to give the agent standing user context; the official-ish community "MCP Server" plugin) let Claude/Copilot treat the vault as agent memory ([community.obsidian.md/plugins/mcp-server](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/mcp-server)). **Claudian** embeds Claude Code as a sidebar inside Obsidian with the vault as working directory. Growing sentiment: for a plain-markdown vault, "Claude Code + grep over files" competes with (and sometimes beats) embedding pipelines ([codeculture.store — Smart Connections vs Copilot vs Claude Code](https://codeculture.store/blogs/developer-culture/obsidian-ai-plugin-comparison-2025)).

## 4. Open-source self-hosted second brains — architectures

| Project | Stack | Retrieval design | Notable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Khoj** ([github.com/khoj-ai/khoj](https://github.com/khoj-ai/khoj), ~30k+ stars) | Django + Gunicorn server, Next.js UI, **Postgres + pgvector** | Embeddings of md/org/PDF/Notion/GitHub docs in pgvector; RAG chat; any LLM (Ollama local or OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini) | Beyond RAG: custom agents, scheduled automations, deep research, web search (SearxNG), code sandbox (Terrarium). 4GB RAM w/ cloud LLMs. The most complete "personal AI server." |
| **Reor** ([github.com/reorproject/reor](https://github.com/reorproject/reor)) | Electron desktop, llama.cpp/Ollama for LLM, **Transformers.js** local embeddings, **LanceDB** embedded vector DB | Every note chunked+embedded on write; related-notes via vector similarity; Q&A = RAG over corpus; semantic search; AI flashcards | Thesis: "AI tools for thought should run models locally by default." Framed as "a RAG app with two generators: the LLM and the human." Cleanest small local-first architecture to copy. |
| **Quivr** ([github.com/QuivrHQ/quivr](https://github.com/QuivrHQ/quivr)) | Python, LangChain, **Supabase/pgvector** (1.6M+ embeddings), Docker | Opinionated RAG core ("focus on your product, not the RAG"); any LLM, any vectorstore (PGVector, Faiss), custom parsers | Pivoted from consumer "second brain" to embeddable RAG engine — itself a data point that consumer chat-with-docs didn't retain users. |
| **AnythingLLM** | Desktop app + server, workspace-per-corpus model | One-click document-chat workspaces, embedded vector store, any LLM | Wins on polish for non-technical doc chat; workspace (not graph) mental model. |

Pattern: **pgvector-in-Postgres is the boring-and-correct choice** for a personal-scale corpus (docs + vectors in one transactional store, search is a SQL query; "you probably don't need a vector database" — [encore.dev/blog/you-probably-dont-need-a-vector-database](https://encore.dev/blog/you-probably-dont-need-a-vector-database)). For a SQLite-based stack like workingmemory, the equivalent is `sqlite-vec`/LanceDB or plain brute-force cosine over a few thousand vectors, which is trivially fast at personal scale.

## 5. Converged AI+PKM patterns — proven vs. hype

**Proven (repeatedly validated by practitioners):**
1. **AI summarization at capture time for triage** — a 150–300 word summary answering "core argument, evidence, conclusion" so you can decide whether to read fully. This is the single highest-value AI feature in a read-later app. It doesn't replace deep reading; it routes attention.
2. **Semantic/embedding search** — finding saves by concept when you've forgotten titles/keywords. Cheap, reliable, works even on messy corpora. Local embedding models (nomic-embed-text) are good enough.
3. **Auto-tagging/classification against a fixed taxonomy** — LLM tagging works well **when constrained to a predefined vocabulary** (e.g., your PARA projects/areas). Unconstrained tag generation produces tag explosion: one study saw 765 hyper-specific one-off tags without a taxonomy vs. 240 reusable ones with ([moreawesome.co](https://moreawesome.co/insights/using-llms-for-automating-taxonomy-tagging/)); retrieval-then-classify pipelines beat free generation ([LLM4Tag, arxiv 2502.13481](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13481v2)). Your AIA2ndBrain PARA classifier is exactly the proven shape.
4. **Voice/transcription capture** — universally cited as the AI feature that removes a real bottleneck.
5. **Grounded Q&A over a bounded source set** (NotebookLM model) — chat over *this document / this week's saves* with citations works far better than chat over your entire life's notes. NotebookLM is loved precisely because it's source-grounded, though even it hallucinates in audio overviews (~13% rate reported) ([tisankan.medium.com](https://tisankan.medium.com/the-notebooklm-hype-is-real-but-so-are-its-limits-9eee519ec3c1)).
6. **Proactive resurfacing / digest** — Readwise's daily review, Mem's "Heads Up," AI weekly digests of what you saved clustered by theme (an explicitly advertised Readwise-MCP use case). Practitioners report quarterly review of AI weekly summaries reveals real patterns ("which goals keep appearing without progress") ([asianefficiency.com](https://www.asianefficiency.com/productivity/automate-weekly-review-ai/)).

**Mixed / conditional:**
7. **RAG chat over your whole vault ("talk to your notes")** — works as a *retrieval shortcut* ("what did I write about X, with sources"), fails as an *analyst*. Questions whose answers span 40 notes ("how has my thinking on pricing evolved?", "find contradictions") get confidently shallow answers from 5 retrieved chunks; naive RAG flattens link structure and throws the graph away; answer quality is capped by note quality ([thinkdifferent.blog](https://www.thinkdifferent.blog/blog/rag-on-your-own-notes-build-a-second-brain-that-actually-answers/), [HN: From zero to a RAG system](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47499356)).
8. **AI flashcard generation** — real demand (RemNote, Recall, Reor all ship it) but quality is the problem: the 2026 Memory Machines benchmark found even the best model produced unusable cards 36% of the time — not hallucinations but structural flaws (ambiguous, wordy, context-dependent) ([evakeiffenheim.substack.com](https://evakeiffenheim.substack.com/p/when-ai-flashcards-pollute-your-anki), [alexejgossmann.com](https://www.alexejgossmann.com/LLMs-for-spaced-repetition/)). Works only with human review in the loop.
9. **AI auto-linking / "surfacing connections"** — Smart Connections' passive related-notes pane is genuinely liked; fully automatic graph-building tends to produce noise.

**Hype / anti-patterns:**
10. **Full auto-distillation replacing engagement** — the sharpest critique: "the human value of distillation isn't the output, it's the process… outsourcing summarization to AI is the cognitive-crutch version: the output is identical, the learning is absent" ([self.md/articles/tiago-forte-second-brain-ai](https://self.md/articles/tiago-forte-second-brain-ai/)). Also the "second job" critique: systems demanding read→highlight→summarize→tag→file→review get abandoned within ~3 months; and "capture without retrieval is hoarding — 10,000 unread highlights is a landfill, not a second brain" ([ronforbes.com](https://www.ronforbes.com/blog/building-your-ai-second-brain), [plaud.ai](https://www.plaud.ai/blogs/articles/second-brain)). Consensus: AI should remove bottlenecks (transcription, triage, filing, resurfacing), not remove the human from reading/thinking.
11. Keyword search rebranded as AI; tag suggestions with no downstream behavior; unconstrained "self-organizing" magic (Mem 1.0's failure mode).

**The RAG-alternative worth knowing — Karpathy's "LLM wiki" (April 2026):** no vector DB, no embeddings — three folders (`raw/` inbox you dump into, `wiki/` an interlinked markdown wiki the LLM compiles and continuously maintains as a "full-time librarian," `output/` for generated reports), with a big-context LLM agent doing compile-time synthesis instead of query-time retrieval. "RAG is O(1) lookup per query; a wiki compounds." Self-healing, auditable, human-readable ([venturebeat.com](https://venturebeat.com/data/karpathy-shares-llm-knowledge-base-architecture-that-bypasses-rag-with-an), [angelo-lima.fr](https://angelo-lima.fr/en/karpathy-second-brain-obsidian-claude-en/)). This maps beautifully onto a read-later app: saves land in `raw/`; a background agent (you already have the Gemini watcher) distills them into an evolving, linked knowledge layer.

## 6. Second brain as personal context for AI agents (the 2025–26 frontier)

Tiago Forte himself now frames the whole field as **"Personal Context Management is replacing Personal Knowledge Management"** — the bottleneck is no longer AI capability but "your ability to give AI the right information at the right time"; notes/files are "your most valuable professional asset" because agent harnesses (Claude Code, Codex) can now act over them ([fortelabs.com/blog/introducing-the-ai-second-brain](https://fortelabs.com/blog/introducing-the-ai-second-brain/)). Concrete manifestations:

- **MCP servers over personal data**: Readwise MCP (reading history), Obsidian MCP servers (vault as agent memory), Tana workspaces queryable by agents. The design move: your app's data becomes a *tool* any LLM client can call. One cited result: stable user context via MCP increased task accuracy 26% and cut token use 90% vs. restating context ([medium — MCPVault](https://medium.com/@ai_transfer_lab/mcpvault-the-claude-skill-that-turns-obsidian-into-a-live-agent-memory-6f3aca3dfc4c)).
- **Agent-memory frameworks**: **Mem0** (~48k stars, $24M raised Oct 2025) — framework-agnostic memory layer: LLM pipeline extracts entities/relations from conversations into a graph + vectors + KV, with conflict detection/merging; **Letta (MemGPT)** — full agent runtime with OS-inspired memory tiers (core/RAM, recall/disk-cache, archival/cold storage) ([vectorize.io/articles/mem0-vs-letta](https://vectorize.io/articles/mem0-vs-letta)). These are the "memory for LLMs" mirror image of PKM: same problem, approached from the agent side.
- Implication for a read app: **what you read is premium personal context.** A read-later app that exposes its library via MCP (search, get-summary, list-recent, tag) instantly becomes memory for every AI tool its owner uses — Burn 451 and Readwise both treat this as a headline feature.

## 7. Synthesis for the "read app" (capture layer, Node.js dev, existing Gemini+PARA watcher, Next.js+SQLite task board)

The community-validated blueprint for an AI-augmented read-later capture layer:

1. **Frictionless capture**: bookmarklet/share-sheet/PWA share target + an email-in address (newsletters) + paste-a-URL; store clean reader-view text (Readability/defuddle extraction), plus YouTube transcripts and PDFs (you already extract PDFs).
2. **AI on ingest (async pipeline, like your file-watcher)**: (a) 150–300-word triage summary + one-line "why this might matter to you"; (b) classification against your **fixed PARA taxonomy** (reuse AIA2ndBrain's classifier — constrained taxonomy is the proven pattern); (c) embedding for semantic search (sqlite-vec or brute-force cosine in SQLite is fine at personal scale); (d) auto-tags from a controlled vocabulary only.
3. **Triage-first reading UX**: inbox → (read now / later / archive / shortlist) states; surface the AI summary *before* the article to force a 30-second keep/kill decision; consider decay/expiry pressure (Burn 451's forcing function) since unbounded queues are the #1 documented failure mode.
4. **Retrieval**: hybrid search (FTS5 BM25 + vector) beats either alone; "chat with this document" (grounded, cite-the-source) rather than "chat with everything."
5. **Resurfacing**: AI weekly digest of saves clustered by theme + spaced resurfacing of highlights; this is what converts a landfill into a second brain.
6. **Agent access**: expose the library via a small MCP server (search, recent, summary, tag) so Claude Code/other agents can use your reading as context — the highest-leverage 2026-era feature and a natural fit with your existing tooling.
7. **Keep the human in the loop for distillation**: AI summaries for triage, human highlights for meaning; optionally Karpathy-style compile-time synthesis (background job that maintains topic pages in markdown from your saves) instead of/alongside RAG.

## Key takeaways
- The read-later market has a genuine opening: Omnivore (2024) and Pocket (2025) both shut down, and no open, hackable, AI-native read-later app has replaced them — Readwise Reader ($9.99/mo, Ghostreader AI, MCP server) is the reference design to study.
- The single most validated AI feature for a read-later app is a 150-300 word summary generated at capture time used for 30-second triage (keep/read/kill) — it routes attention rather than replacing reading.
- LLM auto-tagging works reliably only against a fixed, small taxonomy (like your existing PARA classifier); unconstrained tag generation produces tag explosion (765 one-off tags vs 240 reusable ones in one study) — your AIA2ndBrain Gemini classifier is already the proven pattern.
- 'Chat with all your notes' RAG is a retrieval shortcut, not an analyst: it answers 'what did I save about X' well but fails at synthesis questions spanning many documents; grounded per-document or per-week Q&A with citations (NotebookLM model) is what actually satisfies users.
- The number-one documented failure mode is capture-without-retrieval ('10,000 unread saves is a landfill'); successful designs add resurfacing (AI weekly digest clustered by theme, spaced review of highlights) and even forcing functions like Burn 451's 24-hour expiry.
- At personal scale you don't need a vector database: hybrid search (SQLite FTS5 BM25 + sqlite-vec or brute-force cosine over embeddings) in your existing SQLite stack matches what Khoj (pgvector), Reor (LanceDB), and Obsidian Copilot (Orama) do — and Obsidian Copilot's index-corruption issues warn against fancy embedded stores.
- Keep the human in the loop for distillation: practitioners consistently report AI-written summaries replacing your own engagement is a cognitive crutch (output identical, learning absent), and AI-generated flashcards are ~36% unusable without human review.
- The 2025-26 frontier is the second brain as personal context for AI agents: exposing your reading library via a small MCP server (search, recent, summary, tag) makes it memory for Claude Code and every other AI tool — Readwise and Burn 451 both treat this as a headline feature, and Tiago Forte now frames the whole field as 'Personal Context Management'.
- Karpathy's LLM-wiki pattern (raw/ inbox -> background LLM 'librarian' maintains an interlinked markdown wiki -> no RAG at query time) maps perfectly onto your architecture: your file-watcher already is the background agent; saves could compile into evolving topic pages instead of just being indexed.
- Structure makes AI reliable: Tana's supertags and Capacities' typed objects show that giving the LLM typed fields and controlled vocabularies beats free-form magic — Mem 1.0's unconstrained 'self-organizing' promise was the canonical under-delivery.
- Voice capture with AI transcription and email-in newsletter ingestion are cheap, universally praised capture features; clean reader-view extraction plus YouTube transcripts and PDFs should all land in one unified queue with triage states (inbox/later/archive/shortlist).
- Proven vs hype in one line: summaries-for-triage, semantic search, constrained auto-filing, grounded Q&A, and digest resurfacing are proven; whole-vault chat as analyst, unreviewed flashcard generation, full auto-distillation, and folderless self-organizing magic are the documented gimmicks.
