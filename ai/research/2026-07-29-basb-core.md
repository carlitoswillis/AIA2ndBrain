# Building a Second Brain (BASB) — Canonical Reference

Tiago Forte's *Building a Second Brain* (book, 2022; methodology developed over ~10 years of Forte Labs blog posts and the BASB cohort course) is a Personal Knowledge Management (PKM) system built on one premise: **your brain is for having ideas, not holding them**. The Second Brain is "an external, centralized, digital repository for the things you learn and the resources from which they come." The system's north star is *actionability* — notes exist to be used in creative output, not to be collected.

Primary sources: [fortelabs.com/blog/basboverview/](https://fortelabs.com/blog/basboverview/) (definitive intro guide), [fortelabs.com/blog/para/](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/), [Progressive Summarization](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization-a-practical-technique-for-designing-discoverable-notes/), [Intermediate Packets](https://fortelabs.com/blog/just-in-time-pm-4-intermediate-packets/), [The Secret Power of Read It Later Apps](https://medium.com/praxis-blog/the-secret-power-of-read-it-later-apps-6c75cc37ef42), [Weekly Review is an Operating System](https://fortelabs.com/blog/the-weekly-review-is-an-operating-system/), [Monthly Review is a Systems Check](https://fortelabs.com/blog/the-monthly-review-is-a-systems-check/), [12 Favorite Problems](https://fortelabs.com/blog/12-favorite-problems-how-to-spark-genius-with-the-power-of-open-questions/).

## 1. The CODE Method — Capture, Organize, Distill, Express

Every piece of information flows through four stages that make it progressively more useful. The four steps also map to three maturity stages of Second Brain use: **Remember → Connect → Create**.

### C — Capture: Keep What Resonates
- Save only information that *resonates* — a felt, intuitive signal (pleasure, curiosity, wonder, excitement), **not** an analytical decision. Forte: don't ask *why* it resonates; the ideas that resonate are usually the most unusual, counterintuitive, or potentially useful.
- **Four capture criteria** (from the book, Ch. 4): (1) *Does it inspire me?* (2) *Is it useful?* (raw material for later — stats, diagrams, references), (3) *Is it personal?* (your own thoughts, reflections, memories — no one else has these), (4) *Is it surprising?* (if it doesn't surprise you, you already knew it — echoes Claude Shannon's definition of information).
- **Golden rule: capture no more than ~10% of the original source.** Capture is curation, not hoarding.
- **Knowledge assets** worth capturing — external: highlights, quotes, bookmarks, voice memos, meeting notes, images, takeaways; internal: stories, insights, memories, reflections, musings.
- What *not* to capture: sensitive info, special file formats better kept elsewhere, very large files, actively collaborative docs.
- Capture must take **seconds** — hit share/export/save and move on. Friction kills the habit.

### O — Organize: Save for Actionability
- Organizing question: **"In which project will this be most useful?"** — organize by *use*, not by topic/subject. Placement cascade: Project → (if none) Area → (if none) Resource → (if none) Archive; if nothing fits and nothing resonates, don't keep it.
- Implemented via **PARA** (see §2). Store notes where they'll be useful *soonest*.
- Organizing should take seconds too; a default inbox plus periodic batch-filing (in the weekly review) beats filing perfectly at capture time.

### D — Distill: Find the Essence
- "Notes aren't for collecting, they're for using." Every touch of a note should add value for Future You — chiefly via **Progressive Summarization** (see §3).
- **Distill at the moment of use, not at capture.** Highlighting/summarizing every note up front = "hours of meticulous highlighting with no clear purpose." You distill when you're getting ready to *create* something and the note earns the attention.

### E — Express: Show Your Work
- The entire system's payoff. Knowledge only compounds when turned into output: writing, decisions, code, designs, conversations. Work in **Intermediate Packets** (§4).
- **Four retrieval methods** when starting a creative task: (1) Search, (2) Browse (PARA folders), (3) Tags, (4) Serendipity (encountering notes while doing other things).

## 2. PARA — Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives

A universal folder scheme for *all* digital information (notes app, filesystem, cloud drive, bookmarks, email), organized by **actionability, descending**:

1. **Projects** — short-term efforts you're actively working on, with a *concrete outcome and a deadline/timeframe* ("Complete webpage design," "Renovate the bathroom"). Reviewed most frequently. A healthy list is roughly 10–15 projects.
2. **Areas** — ongoing responsibilities with a *standard to maintain and no end date* (Health, Finances, "Marketing," "Direct reports"). Reviewed less often.
3. **Resources** — topics of interest and reference material for the future (Graphic design, Japanese, Photography). Reviewed only when you decide to act on the topic.
4. **Archives** — inactive items from the other three. Cold storage; never delete, so old material remains searchable and reusable.

Key doctrine:
- **Actionability over subjects.** Not "Psychology"/"Business" folders — folders aligned with *outcomes you're trying to achieve*. All materials for one project live together regardless of discipline.
- **Projects vs. Areas is the critical distinction.** "Hiring" is an endless Area; "Hire 2 backend engineers by Q3" is a Project. Breaking areas into completable projects gives (a) clarity on actual commitments and (b) "a cadence of regular victories."
- **PARA is dynamic.** Items flow between categories constantly (a Resource becomes project fuel; a finished Project moves to Archives; an Archive gets resurrected). Reorganization happens *just in time*, not up front.
- **Mirror the same four folders across every platform** so any tool answers "where does this go?" identically.
- The system "has to give you time, not take time" — the ultimate organizing system is the one that makes action easier to start and finish.

Sources: [fortelabs.com/blog/para/](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/); also *The PARA Method* (standalone book, 2023).

## 3. Progressive Summarization — layered distillation

Technique for making notes **discoverable** (compressed, glanceable) without destroying **understanding** (context). Core tension: "you cannot compress something without losing some of its context." Design for the note's *Future Self reader*, who needs to judge a note's usefulness in seconds.

**The layers** (each in distinct formatting):
- **Layer 0** — the original full source (article, book, video).
- **Layer 1** — the captured excerpt in your notes ("anything that feels insightful, interesting, or useful"; ≤10% of source).
- **Layer 2** — **bold** the best passages of Layer 1.
- **Layer 3** — ==highlight== the best of the bolded ("best of the best").
- **Layer 4** — a short *executive summary in your own words* at the top of the note.
- **Layer 5** — **remix**: transform into new work (blog post, sketch, video, product).

**Empirical calibration (Forte's own ratios):** ~50% of consumed sources get any note at all; ~25% reach Layer 2; ~20% Layer 3; ~5% Layer 4; **<1%** Layer 5. Value follows a power law — spend distillation effort accordingly. "More summarization is *not* better."

**Timing = opportunistic compression:** summarize "in small spurts, spread across time, in the course of other work" — each time you *touch* a note for a real purpose, push it one layer deeper. Never batch-summarize for its own sake.

**Guidelines** ([PS III: Guidelines and Principles](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization-iii-guidelines-and-principles/)):
1. Don't apply all layers to all notes.
2. Use *resonance* as the highlighting criterion (surprising, contradicts assumptions, connects to experience, simplifies, tickles curiosity).
3. **"Design for the worst version of yourself"** — tired, lazy, unmotivated. Simple rules over elaborate taxonomies/tags.
4. Keep notes *glanceable*; over-highlighting (the #1 mistake) dilutes discoverability. Prioritize **recognition over recall**.

Related caution ([Forte on highlighting in Medium/Pocket/Instapaper](https://medium.com/@fortelabs/the-risk-in-using-highlighting-in-medium-or-pocket-or-instapaper-or-anywhere-else-that-is-not-f34299ac8588)): highlights stored *inside* third-party read-later apps are fragile — extract the "hard copy" text into your own notes system and do the layering there, so your investment survives app changes.

## 4. Creative Execution Techniques

### Intermediate Packets (IPs)
Instead of "delivering one big lump of value at the very end, you stage your progress in a series of short, intense sprints, ending each one with a tangible, intermediate deliverable." Five types (book): distilled notes, outtakes, work-in-progress, final deliverables from past projects, documents created by others. Examples: a brainstorm, an outline, a prototype, a draft, a code module, a research doc, a Pinterest board.

Benefits: (1) interruption-proof work (small units, easy resume), (2) earlier/better feedback ("others give much better feedback when included early and the work is clearly in draft stage"), (3) create value in any span of time (5 min or 5 hours), (4) big projects feel less intimidating, (5) **reusability — packets are Lego bricks for future projects**. Source: [Just-in-Time PM #4](https://fortelabs.com/blog/just-in-time-pm-4-intermediate-packets/).

### Archipelago of Ideas (execution technique #1 — never start from a blank page)
Before creating, assemble an outline of pre-existing "islands": quotes, links, your own notes, examples — then write/build by connecting islands into an archipelago. Separates *divergence* (gathering) from *convergence* (composing) — two modes that fail when mixed.

### Hemingway Bridge (technique #2 — carry momentum across sessions)
Hemingway stopped writing only when he knew what came next. End every work session by writing down: next steps, current status, details you'll forget, and your intention for the next session. Makes restarting cheap; pairs naturally with IPs.

### Dial Down the Scope (technique #3 — ship on time by shrinking, not slipping)
When a deadline nears, don't move the date and don't grind harder — **cut scope**. Move the trimmed pieces (unfinished sections, ideas) back into your Second Brain as IPs for a future project. "Ship something small and concrete" — iterate with what you have.

## 5. Twelve Favorite Problems — the capture filter

Borrowed from Richard Feynman (via Gian-Carlo Rota): "You have to keep a dozen of your favorite problems constantly present in your mind... Every time you hear or read a new trick or a new result, test it against each of your twelve problems to see whether it helps."

In BASB, your ~12 open-ended questions are the **standing filter for capture**: when something resonates, it's usually because it speaks to one of your favorite problems. Good favorite problems are: genuinely fascinating to you, open-ended (no single answer), interdisciplinary, personally significant, and scoped enough to guide investigation (e.g. "How do I read more of what I love?", "How can systems replace willpower?"). They turn passive consumption into an active matching process and become containers that organize new knowledge. Sources: [fortelabs.com 12 Favorite Problems](https://fortelabs.com/blog/12-favorite-problems-how-to-spark-genius-with-the-power-of-open-questions/), [My Favorite Favorite Problems](https://fortelabs.com/blog/my-favorite-favorite-problems/) (crowd-sourced examples).

## 6. Review Habits — the maintenance layer

### Weekly Review ("an operating system") — tactical, 15–30 min
Forte's checklist ([full article](https://fortelabs.com/blog/the-weekly-review-is-an-operating-system/)); his condensed 5-point version: **1) Email** — clear inbox, capture new tasks/notes; **2) Calendar** — review ±weeks for new tasks/notes; **3) Desktop/Downloads** — clear; **4) Notes** — clear notes inbox (batch-file 30–50 accumulated notes into PARA); **5) Tasks** — clear task-manager inbox and plan the week. Note: the weekly review *files* notes, it does not distill them — distillation waits for the moment of use.

### Monthly Review ("a systems check") — strategic
([full article](https://fortelabs.com/blog/the-monthly-review-is-a-systems-check/)) Part I, Perspective: review life goals (SMART updates), update project list (archive completed, clarify outcomes, reprioritize), assess areas of responsibility, review personal narrative/vision. Part II, Housekeeping: review Someday/Maybe for promotion, reprioritize tasks (fix "priority drift"), **extract highlights from finished ebooks**, empty digital trash.

### Project checklists (book, Ch. 9)
- **Kickoff:** capture current thinking → review PARA folders for related notes → search across the whole system → move relevant notes into the project folder → create an outline/plan from them.
- **Completion:** mark complete → cross off goal → review IPs produced and file reusable ones → archive the project folder → if merely inactive, add a status note for future resumption.
- **"Noticing" habits:** capture unconventional ideas, overlooked connections, and opportunities to recombine existing IPs as they occur.

## 7. Capture tooling and the role of read-later apps (Forte's own stack)

The [BASB getting-started guide](https://fortelabs.com/blog/basboverview/) and [resource guide](https://www.buildingasecondbrain.com/resources) prescribe: 1) decide what to capture, 2) choose a notes app (pick the one you can start with *right now* with least hassle), **3) choose a capture tool — "start with a read later app"** (named: Instapaper, Pocket, Readwise Reader), 4) set up PARA, 5) identify your 12 favorite problems, then practice PS, make one IP, schedule the weekly review.

Forte's canonical read-later argument ([The Secret Power of 'Read It Later' Apps](https://medium.com/praxis-blog/the-secret-power-of-read-it-later-apps-6c75cc37ef42)) — the design theory behind the category:
- **Separate capture from consumption.** In "collection mode" he command-clicks links into tabs and batch-saves to Pocket *without reading anything*. The temporal buffer between impulse and consumption dramatically improves curation: "decisions... much better aligned with what truly matters."
- **The consumption funnel:** massive intake → time-shifted filtering (**he deletes ~1/3 of the queue unread**) → selective reading → highlights → export to Evernote (via IFTTT: liked articles + highlights auto-flow to a default notebook for later PARA filing). Only the best passages ever reach the permanent notes system.
- **Volume payoff:** in 2014 the system produced 1M+ words read (≈22 books of long-form reading "that would not have happened without a system").
- **Four barriers read-later apps remove:** slow/ad-ridden pages (clean text view), context-mismatch (a curated magazine-like queue you read when the moment suits), asynchrony (progress sync across devices; kills the Zeigarnik open-loop burden), and focus (reading queue kept *separate* from the task manager and the notes archive — "the last thing you want to see when you finally have time to read is the thousands of notes you've collected").
- **Historical stack:** Pocket → Instapaper (after Pocket's Share-to-Evernote broke), highlights piped into Evernote, PARA notebooks, weekly-review filing. He warns highlights left inside read-later apps are fragile; the durable asset is extracted text + layers in your own system.

## 8. Design implications distilled for an AI-augmented read-later app

(Interpretation layer — mapping BASB doctrine onto the capture-tool product:)
- The read-later app *is* BASB's Capture stage plus the top of the Distill funnel. Its jobs: one-tap frictionless save; a queue deliberately separate from notes/tasks; clean reader view; delayed-consumption buffer; easy triage/deletion (celebrate the ~33% unread cull, don't guilt-trip about backlog); highlighting; and reliable **export of text + highlights into the permanent PARA-organized store** (your AIA2ndBrain watcher is exactly the receiving end Forte scripts with IFTTT).
- AI fits BASB where judgment is cheap and mechanical: suggesting PARA/project placement ("in which project is this most useful?"), matching saves against the user's Twelve Favorite Problems, drafting Layer 4 executive summaries *on demand at moment of use* (not eagerly for everything — power-law effort), surfacing serendipitous connections to existing notes/IPs, and powering weekly-review batch triage. AI should *not* replace the resonance judgment at capture — the felt filter is the point — but can flag likely-resonant passages.
- Honor "design for the worst version of yourself": defaults over configuration, seconds-not-minutes interactions, inbox-then-batch rather than file-at-capture.


## Key takeaways
- BASB's explicit getting-started step 3 is 'choose a capture tool — start with a read-later app' (Instapaper/Pocket/Readwise Reader), making the read app the canonical entry point of the whole system.
- The core read-later insight is temporal separation of capture from consumption: one-tap batch saving now, curated reading later — Forte deletes ~1/3 of his queue unread, so easy guilt-free triage/deletion is a first-class feature, not a failure state.
- Capture must take seconds and be filtered by felt 'resonance' plus four criteria (inspiring, useful, personal, surprising), capped at ~10% of the source — AI can flag likely-resonant passages but shouldn't replace the user's intuitive judgment.
- The read-later queue must stay separate from the notes archive and task manager, but reliably export extracted text + highlights into the permanent store (Forte used IFTTT → Evernote; your AIA2ndBrain watcher is that receiving end), because highlights trapped in the reader app are fragile.
- Organize everything by actionability with PARA — the placement question is 'in which project will this be most useful?' cascading Project → Area → Resource → Archive — which is an ideal LLM classification task your Gemini watcher already approximates.
- Distill lazily via Progressive Summarization layers (capture → bold → highlight → executive summary → remix) at the moment of use, not at capture; effort follows a power law (~5% of notes ever get a summary, <1% get remixed), so AI summaries should be on-demand, not eager.
- Twelve Favorite Problems act as a standing capture filter — storing the user's open questions and matching every saved article against them is a natural, high-value AI feature for a read app.
- Output is built from Intermediate Packets (notes, outlines, drafts, code modules) that get reused like Lego; the read app's exported highlight-notes are the rawest IP type feeding Express.
- Execution techniques to support downstream: Archipelago of Ideas (assemble saved notes into outlines, never start blank), Hemingway Bridge (persist next-step state between sessions), Dial Down the Scope (cut scope, bank trimmings as future IPs).
- The weekly review (15-30 min, tactical) batch-files the notes inbox into PARA and clears the reading queue; the monthly review (strategic) updates goals/projects and extracts finished-book highlights — a read app should expose a review/triage mode aligned with these cadences.
- Design for 'the worst version of yourself' — tired, lazy, unmotivated: defaults over taxonomies, glanceable notes, recognition over recall, inbox-then-batch over file-at-capture.
- The system's purpose is Express, not collection ('notes aren't for collecting, they're for using'), so every feature should shorten the path from saved article to creative output.
