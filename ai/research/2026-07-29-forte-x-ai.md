# Tiago Forte on AI/LLMs and the Future of Second Brains (2023–2026)

## Executive framing

Tiago Forte did not double down defensively when ChatGPT arrived — he treated it as an extinction-level event for his own methodology. In early 2023, after teaching ~6,000 students over six years, he **stopped teaching the Building a Second Brain (BASB) cohorts** because "almost overnight, much of what he'd built felt obsolete" and he couldn't authentically keep teaching the same methods. He then spent roughly three years studying AI and relaunched in 2026 with a fully AI-first reformulation: **the second brain is no longer primarily a retrieval system for a human reader — it is a curated context source for AI**. His new umbrella concept is **Personal Context Management (PCM)**, explicitly positioned as the successor to Personal Knowledge Management (PKM).

## 1. The arc: from PKM to Personal Context Management

### "Introducing The AI Second Brain" — fortelabs.com, March 13, 2026 (updated April 14, 2026)
URL: https://fortelabs.com/blog/introducing-the-ai-second-brain/

The manifesto piece. Key positions:

- ChatGPT's release in early 2023 forced him to question whether his methodology was obsolete; three years later he concluded AI reached a pivotal point when systems like **Claude Code** could access files, use tools, and take action — "moving beyond mere conversation."
- Core philosophy: "AI isn't meant to replace human labor or judgment with supposedly 'autonomous' agents. It's meant to amplify human ability."
- **The bottleneck flipped**: it is no longer AI capability, it is *your ability to provide AI with the right information at the right time*. That is Personal Context Management.
- The old BASB metaphor of the second brain as a "digital confidant and thought partner" is now literal reality; consequently "your personal notes and files become your most valuable professional asset."
- He warns against hype: "There is far more nuance, complexity, and troubleshooting required than almost any AI content creator will lead you to believe," and argues social change lags technical breakthroughs.
- He also flags **memory degradation in AI systems** (context loss between sessions) as a problem requiring active management strategies — directly relevant to any app design: persistent user context must live *outside* the model.

### The AI Second Brain course — buildingasecondbrain.com/ai-second-brain
Cohort 1 ran April 15–May 1, 2026 (364 graduates, NPS 70); Cohort 2 runs Sept 17–Oct 8, 2026. A 3-week live program **taught primarily on Claude (including Claude Code)**, adaptable to ChatGPT/Gemini. Curriculum highlights:
- The "Master Prompt" — a "portable operating manual" giving AI your role, values, goals, and standards (living document, sometimes 20+ pages).
- **PARA adapted for AI workflows**; "notetaking systems designed to feed AI systems."
- "AI Board of Advisors" — specialized persona roles (financial strategist, domain expert).
- The pitch line: "better results come from better context," not better prompts or tools; the "Context Gap" is "the skill nobody's teaching." Without your context, AI is "the average of the internet."
- His three-layer context model (articulated in the Recall review, below): **persistent context** (identity, values, style) → **project context** (active work, decisions, research) → **perishable context** (details for one request).

### The Master Prompt Method (2025, YouTube + workshops)
Announced via X in May 2025 (https://x.com/fortelabs/status/1919784471995289806): "The key to unlocking AI's full potential… By giving your AI tools the full context about you and your business." Developed with entrepreneur Hayden Miyamoto; there is a YouTube playlist "AI for Business: The Master Prompt Method" and a related B2B program (**Second Brain Enterprise**, ~200 participants, $1.5M sales, which he wound down in 2025 because B2B didn't fit his individual-focused brand — per "My 2025 Annual Review," https://fortelabs.com/blog/tiago-fortes-2025-annual-review/).

## 2. Does distillation still matter if LLMs summarize on demand? (His direct answer: yes, more than ever)

### "How to Summarize Books Using ChatGPT: 7 Experiments in AI Distillation" — May 12, 2023
URL: https://fortelabs.com/blog/how-to-summarize-books-using-chatgpt/

His earliest and most empirical treatment. He ran seven experiments summarizing Tom DeMarco's *Slack* and found:
- Raw ChatGPT summaries were generic and cliché-ridden; persona prompts helped tone but introduced hallucinations.
- Supplying **his own 1,618 words of highlights** dramatically improved quality; supplying highlights **organized into an outline** produced the best result.
- Punchline quotes: "ChatGPT is always tending toward cliché, which by definition is the 'aggregate average' of the greatest number of writers on which it was trained." And the thesis that anchors his whole AI-era position: **"In a world of infinite, instantaneous summarization, the one with the most unique data to summarize wins."**
- Human highlighting captures "key details, subtle distinctions, and gestalts of meaning… that can't (yet) be reproduced by even the latest language models."

Companion piece: "How to Create an AI Style Guide: Write With ChatGPT in Your Own Voice" (Aug 5, 2023) — codifying your voice as reusable context, an early precursor of the Master Prompt.

### "Will Artificial Intelligence Replace the Need for Second Brains Entirely?" — May 6, 2024
URL: https://fortelabs.com/blog/will-artificial-intelligence-replace-the-need-for-second-brains-entirely/

His canonical rebuttal to "why keep notes at all?" Mapped onto his CODE framework (Capture, Organize, Distill, Express):
- **Organize and Distill (the middle) get automated/cheap**: you can "dump a morass of text into a prompt window"; LLMs save "dozens of hours of formerly manual work" on summarization.
- **Capture and Express (the bookends) become MORE important**: AI cannot go out into the world and decide what's worth saving, and human direction/synthesis still shapes final output.
- Conclusion: "AI concentrates human creativity at the beginning and end of the creative process." The second brain survives because "the data we put into it has to come from somewhere, and the AI's outputs have to go somewhere" — it is the input repository and output staging area.

**Design implication he draws explicitly**: the read-later/capture layer is the part of the stack whose value AI *raises*, not erodes.

## 3. The second brain as context source for AI (2026 articles)

### "Why PARA Is the Key to the AI Era" — April 13, 2026
URL: https://fortelabs.com/blog/why-para-is-the-key-to-the-ai-era/

- File organization is becoming *more* essential, not obsolete: LLMs have limited context windows and can't efficiently hunt across scattered platforms; well-organized PARA folders act as **"pre-assembled bundles of context"** matching how humans actually work.
- "Your file history becomes a treasure trove of context that's totally unique to you."
- **Local files beat cloud storage for AI work**: faster retrieval, better reliability, lower token costs.
- Search finds individual matches; PARA structure provides *meaningful, action-oriented context bundles*. (Directly validates the user's existing PARA-classifying file-watcher.)

### "The Ultimate Guide to Claude Connectors" — June 29, 2026
URL: https://fortelabs.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-claude-connectors/

- Calls Anthropic's **MCP** (released late 2024) "a monumental moment in the history of modern AI"; connectors are the "connective tissue" reconnecting AI to data that migrated from file systems into cloud apps over 20 years.
- He personally connected Claude to Google Calendar, Gmail/Superhuman, **Readwise (30,000+ highlights)**, Google Drive, Notion, Fathom, Circle, Kit, Mercury, QuickBooks, Apple Messages, Evernote, Gamma.
- Caution: "AI doesn't read everything — it samples" (~0.5–5% of available data); and the bottleneck remains human judgment — "the interpretation of what the data means."
- Value comes from pushing past analysis into "concrete recommendations, visual artifacts, or new directions."

### "Is Recall the Second Brain for the AI Era?" — July 9, 2026
URL: https://fortelabs.com/blog/is-recall-the-second-brain-for-the-ai-era/

His most concrete spec for an AI-era read-later/second-brain app (reviewing the app Recall, which saves/summarizes articles, videos, podcasts, PDFs and lets you chat with your library):
- **Praised**: fast web clipper with instant summaries; **grounded chat** (AI answers anchored to your library, with web access); YouTube transcript extraction with timestamps; **spaced-repetition quizzes generated from saved content**.
- **Criticized**: graph view ("looked impressive but proved overwhelmingly unhelpful"); poor file-format import; initially **no persistent user context between conversations** (Recall later added "Personas").
- His ideal AI-era second brain app: (1) effortlessly captures and organizes what you read/watch; (2) grounds AI conversations in personal knowledge while allowing web search; (3) preserves identity via persistent context / master prompt; (4) **integrates with existing AI tools like Claude** rather than being a silo.
- Quotes: "The quality of your context beats the cleverness of the model." "What you're really curating isn't data. It's you."

### "Finding Alpha: What's Worth Consuming in the Age of AI" — July 22, 2026
URL: https://fortelabs.com/blog/finding-alpha-whats-worth-consuming-in-the-age-of-ai/

His consumption/capture strategy for the AI era. AI is an "index fund for human knowledge" — efficient at delivering the average; **"alpha" comes from what AI can't replicate**: "Every answer you get from an LLM is the same as anyone else would get. Therefore, AI on its own cannot be the source of any sustainable advantage."
- Four strategies: **move upstream** (raw/primary sources over derivative content); **hunger for detail** ("AI not only compresses your specifics into generalities, it also expands your generalities into even more generic ones"); **seek disorder** (serendipity lives in messy collection, not rigid taxonomies); **become outcome-oriented** (filter by concrete goals).
- Content rising in value: primary sources, frontier research, personal/private data, embodied perspective, long-form deep dives, emotionally resonant material. Declining: SEO listicles, hot takes, derivative recycled content, short-form addictive video.
- **Read-later apps as filter**: he uses Readwise Reader to insert temporal distance between discovery and consumption — "usually find that half the items don't deserve my attention at all. An excellent filter!"
- Capture doctrine: preserve the "exact words that the original source used," keeping "the most raw, unfiltered version of an idea possible" (don't let the capture pipeline paraphrase — a caution for AI-summarize-on-save designs).

## 4. His elevated view of read-later apps specifically

- X post, April 2025 (https://x.com/fortelabs/status/1910369207755190600): "I'm increasingly starting to think that my **most important productivity tool is my read-later app** (I use @ReadwiseReader)… surprising to me, as for years I've framed a read-later app as an optional tool of secondary importance." He frames it as "creating your own personalized content feed that is **air-gapped from any external, algorithmic timeline** controlled by a corporation" — the save button removes your brain from "ads, clickbait, hype, ragebait" and populates a private timeline of "deeper, more truthful, substantive ideas," time-shifted to when you can read thoughtfully.
- Foundational older piece: "The Secret Power of 'Read It Later' Apps" (Praxis/Medium, ~2015; https://medium.com/praxis-blog/the-secret-power-of-read-it-later-apps-6c75cc37ef42) — read-later apps separate discovery from consumption, enabling batching and attention control; this is why BASB step 3 says "start with a read later app."
- App landscape review: "Test-Driving a New Generation of Second Brain Apps: Obsidian, Tana, and Mem" (Nov 20, 2023; https://fortelabs.com/blog/test-driving-a-new-generation-of-second-brain-apps-obsidian-tana-and-mem/). He rejected all three as daily drivers (Obsidian too IDE-like/complex, Tana no full mobile app, Mem slow with an underpowered AI chat) and predicted **workflow segmentation instead of "one app to rule them all"** — dedicated capture apps alongside specialized tools for distilling and expressing. He stressed unglamorous breadth of capture methods (Evernote's eight ingestion paths) as the hard, undervalued part.
- Other capture tooling he endorses: Readwise (highlight sync), **Snipd** (podcast transcript + audio snippet capture), Otter (voice), NotebookLM for learning/research (his NotebookLM walkthrough video broke 1M views in 2025 and gained him 31k subscribers — see 2025 Annual Review).

## 6. Direct implications for an AI-augmented read-later app (grounded in his positions)

1. Capture must preserve **verbatim source text** and highlights; AI summaries should sit alongside, never replace, the raw material.
2. The app's chat/AI features should be **grounded in the user's library** (his top-praised Recall feature) with optional web augmentation.
3. Ship **persistent user context** (master-prompt/persona layer) — its absence was his biggest criticism of Recall.
4. Organize saves into **PARA-style, project-oriented context bundles**, ideally as local files — "pre-assembled bundles of context" for LLMs; this dovetails with the user's existing AIA2ndBrain PARA classifier.
5. Expose the library to external AI tools (e.g., an **MCP server**) rather than building a walled garden — "integrates seamlessly with existing AI tools like Claude."
6. Build the queue as an **air-gapped, algorithm-free personal feed** with time-shifting and a triage step (he deletes ~half on review — the delay itself is the filter).
7. Skip graph views; consider **spaced-repetition/quiz generation** from saved content, which he explicitly praised.

## Key takeaways
- Forte's core AI-era thesis: 'In a world of infinite, instantaneous summarization, the one with the most unique data to summarize wins' — captured raw material, not summaries, is the moat.
- He replaced Personal Knowledge Management with Personal Context Management (PCM): the second brain's new job is feeding AI 'the right information, in the right format, at the right time', because 'the quality of your context beats the cleverness of the model.'
- AI automates the middle of CODE (Organize, Distill) but makes the bookends — Capture and Express — MORE valuable, so a read-later capture app is the part of the stack AI strengthens rather than obsoletes.
- In April 2025 he declared his read-later app (Readwise Reader) his single most important productivity tool: an 'air-gapped', algorithm-free personal feed whose built-in delay acts as a filter (he deletes about half of saved items on review).
- Capture should preserve the exact verbatim words of the source ('the most raw, unfiltered version of an idea possible'); AI-generated summaries tend toward cliché and should augment, never replace, originals.
- His ideal AI-era app (from the July 2026 Recall review): effortless capture + AI chat grounded in your own library + persistent user identity/master-prompt context + open integration with tools like Claude; he found graph views useless and praised spaced-repetition quizzes from saved content.
- PARA matters more with AI, not less: organized (ideally local) folders act as 'pre-assembled bundles of context' that fit LLM context windows — validating the existing AIA2ndBrain PARA classifier as AI plumbing.
- He calls MCP 'a monumental moment in modern AI' and connects Claude to Readwise, Notion, Gmail, etc. — implying a read-later app should expose its library via MCP rather than be a walled garden.
- The Master Prompt Method (2025): a living 20+ page document of role, values, goals, and style that gives every AI interaction persistent personal context — a feature worth building in (Recall's biggest gap was no persistent context).
- He killed his flagship BASB cohort in early 2023 because ChatGPT made parts feel obsolete, then relaunched in 2026 as the Claude-based 'AI Second Brain' course — evolution, not abandonment, of BASB/PARA.
- 'Every answer you get from an LLM is the same as anyone else would get' — so the app should optimize for capturing high-alpha content (primary sources, personal data, long-form, frontier work) over derivative SEO/short-form content.
- He predicts workflow segmentation over one-app-to-rule-them-all — a dedicated capture/read-later app feeding distill and express tools is the architecture he expects, and broad low-friction ingestion paths are the hard, undervalued part.
